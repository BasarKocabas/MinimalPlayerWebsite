Mono Privacy Policy

Effective October 9, 2026

Mono is a music player for the audio files on your device. It is built to work entirely offline: the app has no internet permission, so it cannot send anything to the developer or anyone else.

What the app reads

  • Your audio files, with your permission, to show and play your music. This includes their tags (title, artist, album), cover art and embedded lyrics.

What the app stores, and where

Everything stays on your device:

  • Your playlists, albums, favorites, play history, queue and settings, in the app's private storage.
  • Automatic backups of that library, saved as files in the Documents/Mono folder on your device, so you can restore it after reinstalling.
  • A crash report, if the app ever crashes, in the app's private storage.

What leaves your device

Nothing is collected or shared by the developer. There are no accounts, ads, analytics or trackers.

  • Crash reports are sent only if you choose Share crash report in Settings. You pick the app (for example, email) and see the report before sending it. It contains the app version, your phone model and Android version, the time of the crash and technical error details.
  • Android backup. If you have Android's backup turned on, Android may include the app's settings and library in your Google account backup. Google provides and controls this; the developer cannot access it.

Permissions

  • Music and audio / storage – to find and play your audio files, and to save backups.
  • Notifications – to show the playback controls.
  • Foreground service and wake lock – to keep playing when the screen is off.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app removes its private data. Backups in Documents/Mono stay until you delete that folder, so you can restore your library later.

Children

The app does not collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
